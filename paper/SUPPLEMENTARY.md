## Note S1 — The capacity theorem

### Statement

Let a benchmark score `n` items, each method receiving a score that is an
average over those items and therefore lies on the grid of denominator `n`. Let
the annotation be wrong on at most a fraction `ε` of items (a worst-case budget,
not a probability model), and let `δ ≥ 0` be the smallest score difference worth
calling a difference. Write `c = max(δ, 2ε)`. Then any family of methods that
the benchmark places in a *certified* total order has cardinality at most

    k(n, ε, δ) = n / (⌊c·n⌋ + 1) + 1.

`BenchmarkCapacity.card_le_benchCapacity`, Lean 4, sorry-free, axioms
`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`.

**Sharpness.** `benchCapacity_attained` constructs a family of exactly `k`
methods that the benchmark does resolve, so `k` is the capacity and not merely
an upper bound. The theorem and its attainment instance are given in three score
models — values on the grid `{0, 1/n, …, 1}`, integer error counts in `{0,…,N}`,
and real scores in `[0, R]` — because the first is what CAID reports, the second
is what a thresholded metric reports, and the third is what a referee will ask
about.

**The n-free corollary.** `benchCapacity_noise_only`: with `δ = 0`,

    k ≤ ⌈1/(2ε)⌉    for every n.

Disorder-PDB has 233 targets and Disorder-NOX 178; both give 7. CAID2's
Disorder-PDB has 256 and also gives 7. **Collecting targets does not buy
resolution the labels lack.**

### Proof outline

The scores of a certified family must be pairwise separated by more than the
noise can move them, which is `c` in the units of the score. On a grid of
denominator `n`, values separated by more than `c` are separated by at least
`⌊c·n⌋ + 1` grid steps, and `[0, 1]` contains `n` steps. Counting the maximum
number of points one can place on `n` steps at that spacing gives the bound; the
attainment instance places them exactly at the spacing.

### The converse, which is what makes this impossibility rather than low power

`unresolvable_pair`. A benchmark observes the **annotation**, not the truth.
Every labelling within the noise budget of the annotation is a consistent
truth. For two methods whose measured scores differ by less than the budget, the
proof *constructs two such truths* — one under which method A is strictly better
and one under which B is — both compatible with every observation the benchmark
recorded.

The ordering is therefore **not a function of the data**. This is the reason no
amount of resampling helps: a bootstrap, a permutation test, leave-one-out and
Monte Carlo all estimate the sampling distribution of a statistic computed from
the same observations, and the ordering is not determined by those observations.
More resamples shrink the Monte Carlo error in locating a p-value and leave the
p-value itself alone. **Only new data or better labels move this bound.**

`over_capacity_has_close_pair` closes the loop: once the entry list exceeds the
capacity, a pair inside the budget necessarily exists. With 117 entrants and a
capacity of 7, CAID3 is over capacity by a factor of seventeen.

### Scope, stated rather than assumed

- The noise model is a **worst-case budget**, not a probability model. That is
  precisely why the converse is a hard impossibility rather than a power
  statement; a stochastic model would give a different and weaker theorem.
- "At capacity" is the maximum size of a **certifiable family**. It is never
  promoted to a claim that such a family has been certified.
- `ε = 0.0801` is measured on 160 of 319 Disorder-PDB targets — those with a
  usable MobiDB record — pooled over 61,013 residues. The 159 remaining targets
  are excluded from the estimate, not assumed noise-free.
- `δ = 0` throughout. Any non-zero effect-size threshold only lowers capacity.
- The theorem bounds what a benchmark can certify about **error-count or
  averaged-score** rankings. It does not bound a *paired* ranking statistic,
  which is the loophole Note S2 exploits deliberately and openly.

## Note S2 — The second-order identity

### Statement

Let `T ⊆ ι` be the truth on one protein (the disordered residues) and `L ⊆ ι`
the annotation. A pair `(p, q)` with `p ∈ T`, `q ∉ T` is **discordant** if the
annotation orders it the other way: `p ∉ L` and `q ∈ L`. Write `d = |T \ L|`
(down-flips: truly disordered, annotated ordered) and `u = |L \ T|` (up-flips).
Then

    discordant(T, L) = 2 · d · u        exactly,
    noise(T, L)      = d + u.

The factor 2 counts each unordered pair in both directions, matching the
convention in which AUC counts ordered pairs.

**Corollary (AM–GM).** `d · u ≤ ((d+u)/2)²`, hence

    discordant(T, L) ≤ noise(T, L)² / 2,

and as rates over `n` residues with balanced classes,

    ε_pair ≤ 2 · ε_label².

**Corollary (capacity).** Substituting into `benchCapacity_noise_only`, a
benchmark scored on within-group ordered pairs has capacity

    k ≤ ⌈1 / (4 ε_label²)⌉,

quadratic in label quality rather than linear.

Formalisation status: `discordant_eq_flip_product`, `discordant_le_noise_sq`
and `pairwise_capacity_quadratic` are in progress. The first is a counting
identity, the second is AM–GM applied to it, the third is a substitution. They
are stated here as mathematics used ahead of its formalisation, and flagged as
such in the main text.

### Measurement

MobiDB publishes the missing-residue call of each individual deposited structure
(`derived-missing_residues-mobi-{PDBID}_{CHAIN}`). For each protein we enumerate
all pairs of its structures and restrict to residues both cover.

| | pooled | median protein |
|---|---:|---:|
| `ε_label` | 0.0651 | 0.0342 |
| `ε_pair` | 0.0070 | 0.0006 |
| ratio | 9.32 | 57 |
| **bound** `2ε²` | **0.00848** | 0.00234 |

The bound holds pooled and is tight to 20%. Per-protein values for all 159
targets with ≥2 structures are in **Table S7**.

Coverage per structure is approximated by the span of its annotated region,
because MobiDB publishes per-structure *missing* regions and not per-structure
coverage. Residues outside a structure's span are treated as undetermined rather
than observed — the conservative direction, since counting them as observed
would manufacture disagreement from absence.

### The mechanism this replaced, and why it is recorded

An earlier version of this argument attributed the gap to **spatial
correlation**: disorder does not flip residue by residue, it flips in regions,
so pairs inside a flipping region keep their relation and only pairs crossing a
boundary reverse. For compact blocks of length `L` in a one-dimensional chain
the boundary-to-area ratio is `O(1/L)`, predicting `ε_pair/ε_label ≈ 1/L`.

The block statistics are real. Across 7,100 disagreement blocks in 147 proteins:

| | residues |
|---|---:|
| median block length | 3 |
| mean | 7.8 |
| **mass-weighted mean** | **73.8** |
| 90th percentile | 13 |
| longest | 472 |

67.4% of context-dependent residues sit in blocks of ten or more, so disorder
does flip in regions. But `1/73.8 = 0.0136` against a measured ratio of
**0.1075** — the correlated-block mechanism **over-predicts the reduction
eightfold**. 31% of blocks are single residues and those contribute discordance
at the full label rate; a mass-weighted mean is the wrong summary of the block
size distribution for this purpose.

The second-order identity needs no correlation assumption at all and predicts
0.00848 against a measured 0.0070. We record the refuted mechanism because the
same measurement that confirmed the right one refuted it, and because "pairwise
scoring works because disorder is spatially correlated" is the explanation a
reader will reach for and it is wrong.

## Note S3 — The protocol, worked

### Specification

As in the main text, steps 1–6. Two points deserve expansion.

**Why the unweighted mean (step 3).** `AUC_within` as it appears in the
decomposition identity is the **pair-weighted** mean of per-target AUCs —
weighted by each target's own positive×negative pair count — because the
identity does not close otherwise. Pair weighting lets a handful of long chains
carry the number. The protocol therefore scores the **unweighted** mean: one
protein, one vote. Both are calibration-invariant, since the invariance holds
target by target before any averaging; the relevant theorem is the per-target
form

```lean
theorem auc_target_strictMono_invariant (f : ℝ → ℝ) (hf : StrictMono f) :
    aucOn t (f ∘ s) = aucOn t s
```

from which the weighted and unweighted means both follow. The published theorem
`auc_within_strictMono_invariant` is stated for the pair-weighted form, so the
per-target statement is what the protocol should cite and it is pending
formalisation. This distinction is recorded rather than elided.

**Why coverage is a gate and not a covariate (step 1).** Declining a target
raises a within-protein score, because the targets methods decline are harder
(Table S9). A method that answers 87% of Linker is not scored on Linker; it is
scored on an easier benchmark with the same name.

### Worked example: what the protocol refuses to order

Disorder-PDB, 120 entered, 60 eligible, 233 two-class targets, ε_pair = 0.0070,
capacity ⌈1/(2·0.0070)⌉ = 72.

The leader, DisorderNet-pbias at 0.9512, is separated at the Holm-corrected
Wilcoxon margin from **55 of the 59** other eligible methods. The four it is not
separated from are reported as tied with it at rank 1 — not as ranks 2, 3, 4 —
and that is the whole of the protocol's honesty mechanism. The residue-level
protocol, on the same data, can certify **seven** orderings in total.

## Note S4 — Pre-registered variants and their outcomes

Every architectural idea in this project was registered before its run, with
floors, endpoints and a decision rule fixed in advance. **Five were rejected.**
They are reported here in full because the capacity result explains them, and
because a paper that reports only the variants that worked is the failure mode
this project was built to avoid.

| # | variant | registered in | primary endpoint, as registered | measured | outcome |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **Private trunk** — detach the binding tasks from the shared trunk | PREREG_4 | Binding-IDR pooled ≥ 0.5900 | 0.5800 | **REJECTED**, plus three floors failed |
| 2 | **Backbone handedness** — two chirality channels from Cα virtual torsions | PREREG_5, _6 | A/B against a regime-matched control on the fixed holdout | null on every task | **REJECTED** — the gate opened 22× over threshold and the A/B saw nothing |
| 3 | **Motif-scale read-out** — a narrow private stack for the binding tasks | PREREG_7 | beat `mt_control` on pooled Binding-IDR | 0.5281 vs 0.5422 (−0.0141) | **REJECTED** |
| 4 | **Distribution matching** — a W₁ term on the protein-level score distribution | PREREG_8 | beat `mt_control` on **between-protein** Disorder-NOX | 0.8564 vs 0.8600 (−0.0036) | **REJECTED** |
| 5 | **Within-protein ranking loss** — optimise the calibration-invariant part directly | PREREG_9 | beat `mt_control` on **within-protein** Disorder-NOX | 0.8500 vs 0.8511 (−0.0011) | **REJECTED** |
| 6 | **Ensemble** — equal-weight rank fusion, membership chosen on the holdout | PREREG_10 | beat PUNCH2 on pooled Disorder-PDB | +0.0062, p = 0.120 | **not significant** |

Endpoints 3–5 were computed for this paper (job 30098857,
`results/caid3/registered_endpoints.py`), because each registration states its
endpoint on a statistic the CAID3 evaluator does not write and each had been
filed as rejected on the pooled score instead. **All three fail on the statistic
they were registered on, and not one of the fifteen paired per-target
comparisons against the control survives Holm** (smallest adjusted p = 0.345).

The two mechanism predictions are the sharper part. PREREG_8 said the
distribution-matching term should move the **between**-protein component,
because the term is permutation-invariant within a protein and therefore cannot
touch the within-protein ordering. PREREG_9 said the ranking surrogate should
move the **within**-protein component, tested directly rather than inferred from
a pooled number. Each registration named the quantity its own mechanism had to
move; neither moved it.

**An unregistered finding from the same computation.** On Binding-IDR,
restricting to the 42 targets that carry both classes among evaluated residues
reverses the sign of all three comparisons: `mt_motif` −0.0141 → **+0.0480**,
`mt_wass` −0.0308 → **+0.0535**, `mt_rank` −0.0050 → **+0.0117**. Ten of the 52
targets carry one class, and a single-class target contributes no within-protein
pair at all — every pair it enters is between-protein. The whole of each
variant's apparent regression lives in targets that carry no residue-level
question. The registered endpoints still fail, because they were registered on
the pooled statistic and that is the statistic that decides them; but the reason
they fail is not that the variants are worse at the residue-level question. This
is the paper's thesis appearing where nobody put it.

The chirality result is the most instructive. The registered gate — a
training-free handedness signal must beat an achiral control by ≥0.010
within-protein — opened by a factor of twenty-two (+0.2256 on Disorder-PDB,
+0.2529 on Disorder-NOX), with the fitted direction the one polymer physics
predicts: disordered residues less right-handed (mean 0.074) than ordered
(0.211), and 61.3% of torsions right-handed overall, as L-amino-acid proteins
should give. **And the A/B against a regime-matched control was null on every
task** (Disorder-PDB +0.0028, p = 0.087; Disorder-NOX +0.0019, p = 0.893;
Binding +0.0496, p = 0.200). The signal is real and the backbone already
carries it.

**Five architectures and an ensemble, each pre-registered, all failing to
separate from PUNCH2 on pooled AUC** (+0.0043, +0.0019, +0.0035, +0.0063,
+0.0062) is exactly what a benchmark over its capacity produces. We were trying
to resolve a pair the benchmark cannot resolve. On the calibration-invariant
axis the same comparison separates on all five references (main text, Fig. 4a).

## Note S5 — Errors found and corrected

Kept in full, because they are the reason to believe the rest, and because each
was caught by a measurement rather than by review.

| what looked true | what was true | what caught it |
|---|---|---|
| Ten CAID3 numbers | the evaluator built the head with default dilations while the checkpoints were trained WIDE; dilation changes no weight shape, so `load_state_dict(strict=True)` accepted it silently and computed a different function at under a third of the trained context | re-deriving the receptive field from the checkpoint's own recorded flags |
| Homologues removed from the validation holdout | `homologous = set(hits)` was a set of `(query_index, subject_index, identity)` triples, so `id not in homologous` was always true; not one homologue left training while the log printed a plausible count | a log line reporting 1,157 held out against 2,538 reported homologues |
| "the head has no channel for protein-level information; the receptive field is 213 residues" | GroupNorm pools over the length axis, so the dependency span is **global**; perturbing residue 0 moves the logit at residue 400 | `lite_head.measured_dependency_span`, written to test the claim |
| pairwise discordance is low because noise is block-correlated, `≈1/L` | the mechanism is second-order, `2·d·u`; `1/L` over-predicts the reduction eightfold | measuring both the block distribution and the ratio |
| AlphaFold-rsa's disordered-residue coverage collapses to 0.527 and it fails the risk guarantee | artefact of scoring our models on 186 chains and the baselines on 61, with different splits; on identical chains rsa covers 0.994 and achieves the guarantee | matching the chain sets |
| every Linker method must flag 100% of the protein | `α` below `1/(n+1)`: arithmetic about *n*, not a fact about any method. Now an explicit refusal by name | the uniformity of the result |
| a dihedral implementation passes its mirror test | it was wrong twice — a 180° offset, then a sign inversion — and the mirror test passed both times | an independently written second implementation |
| a source-text test passes, so the code path works | `LEADERS[task]` is a 3-tuple unpacked as a pair; the test asserted on source text and the code raised at runtime | running it |
| p = 0.0 between two identical methods | both bootstrap tails must carry the ties | comparing a method with itself |
| a certified irreducible-error ranking | `crossed_bound_loose_unbounded`: the greedy crossed-matching certificate is loose by an unbounded factor, because the obstruction is cyclic across three proteins and the certificate is pairwise | the Lean development, which proved the negative we did not ask for |
| "we have never beaten PUNCH2 significantly", stated four times | `mt_pbias` beats PUNCH2 on pooled Disorder-PDB by +0.0083, 95% CI [+0.0010, +0.0176], p = 0.0226. The checkpoint had been filed as rejected by its own pre-registration on three other benchmarks, so its Disorder-PDB comparison stopped being quoted at all | re-reading every checkpoint's result file |

## Table S11 — Certification frontier

`LabelNoise.ranking_certified` turns a margin into a statement about which
method is better *in truth*: a margin above `2·ε·n` certifies the ranking at
annotation error rate `ε`. Because the criterion is monotone in `ε`, the whole
answer is a frontier and no point estimate of `ε` is needed.

| assumed annotation error | certified vs 45 others, Disorder-PDB | Disorder-NOX |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.25% | 43 | 45 |
| 1% | 41 | 44 |
| **2%** | **39** | **43** |
| 3% | 34 | 34 |
| 5% | 21 | 9 |
| **8.01% (measured)** | **15** | **4** |

At the measured rate, **30 of 45** comparisons on Disorder-PDB and **41 of 45**
on Disorder-NOX are inside the annotation noise — not unresolved by this
analysis, but unresolvable.

The **breakdown rate** of a comparison, `margin/(2n)`, is the annotation error
rate that would have to be exceeded before that ranking could be an artefact.
For the closest comparison it is 0.232% on Disorder-PDB (PUNCH2, 460 residues)
and 0.450% on Disorder-NOX; median across the field, 4.78% and 3.71%.
