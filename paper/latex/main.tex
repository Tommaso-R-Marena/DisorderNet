\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Benchmark rankings decide which methods a field adopts, and are treated as data.
We prove they usually are not. A benchmark whose annotations are wrong
at rate $\eps$ can place at most $\lceil 1/2\eps \rceil$ methods in a certifiable
order --- however many enter, however analysed --- and beyond that the ordering is not a
function of the data. At published label-error rates ImageNet's capacity is nine and
QuickDraw's five; their leaderboards rank hundreds. Escape depends on the statistic: an average of per-item losses admits none, but
a ranking statistic over within-group pairs is corrupted only when two labels
flip in opposite directions --- an exactly second-order event. On the Critical Assessment of Intrinsic Disorder, where
$\eps$ is measurable from repeat determinations of one protein, that raises
capacity from 8 to 51 of 117 entrants. We specify the protocol, re-score the field, and show the ranking reproduces on
a held-out round. Every theorem is machine-checked in Lean~4.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{1em}

% =====================================================================
\section*{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

A benchmark is the field's instrument for deciding what works. It is also a
measurement, and every measurement has a resolution --- but no benchmark
publishes one. Leaderboards rank hundreds of methods to four decimal places on
labels that are known to be wrong at rates of one to ten percent.

We prove that the resolution exists and compute it. A benchmark whose score is
an average over $n$ items, with annotations wrong on at most a fraction $\eps$
of them, can place at most $\lceil 1/2\eps \rceil$ methods in a certifiable
order, \emph{however many are entered}. The bound does not improve with more
items, and it is attained. The converse is what makes it an impossibility rather
than a power calculation: past that count, for two close methods we construct
two ground truths, each consistent with every observation the benchmark
recorded, one favouring each. No bootstrap, permutation test, cross-validation
or Monte Carlo recovers an ordering that is not a function of the data.

Label error rates have been measured for the benchmarks the field uses.
Northcutt \emph{et al.}\cite{northcutt} ran confident learning over ten test
sets and had every flagged item checked by human annotators, finding validated
rates from 0.15\% (MNIST) to 10.12\% (QuickDraw) --- lower bounds, which makes
the resulting capacities upper bounds: \textbf{nine} for ImageNet and for
CIFAR-100, five for QuickDraw (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}d). They showed
empirically that correcting the labels reorders these leaderboards; the bound
explains why that is not a curable defect of any analysis.

Whether a benchmark can do better depends on the statistic it chose, which is
the paper's second result. An average of per-item losses admits no escape: an
adversarial placement of the same error budget moves the score by the full
$\eps$. A \emph{ranking} statistic over pairs within a group is corrupted only
when \emph{both} members of a pair are mis-annotated, in opposite directions ---
a structural constraint, not a distributional assumption, making the noise
exactly second-order.

We work this out on the Critical Assessment of Intrinsic Disorder
(CAID)\cite{caid1, caid2}, where the annotation --- residues present in a
protein's sequence but absent from its solved structure --- can be measured
against itself, because many proteins have been solved more than once. That
gives $\eps$ directly rather than by inference, and it gives the pairwise rate
directly too. CAID scores 117 entrants by pooling every residue of every protein
into one area under the ROC curve. Its capacity on that statistic is seven. On
within-protein pairs it is 51.

The disorder problem also exposes what the pooled statistic is measuring. Every
positive--negative pair in it is either within one protein or between two, so it
decomposes exactly,
\[
  \mathrm{AUC}_{\text{pooled}}
    = w_{\text{within}}\,\mathrm{AUC}_{\text{within}}
    + w_{\text{between}}\,\mathrm{AUC}_{\text{between}},
\]
and on CAID3 the within-protein weight is 0.51\%--2.9\%. The reported number is
97\%--99.5\% a question about whole chains, not about residues --- and on three
of five benchmarks that changes who wins.

\section*{Results}
% =====================================================================

\subsection*{CAID's statistic is 97--99.5\% a between-protein question}

The decomposition is exact, and its first term is more than bookkeeping:
$\mathrm{AUC}_{\text{within}}$ is invariant under any per-protein strictly
monotone recalibration (\code{auc\_within\_strictMono\_invariant}), and the
entire effect of such a recalibration falls in the other term
(\code{auc\_pooled\_shift\_diff}). The split is a partition of CAID's statistic
into the part no chain-by-chain rescoring can touch and the part that is nothing
else (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}a; Supplementary Table~S1).

On three of five CAID3 benchmarks the method the challenge declares best is
21st to 25th at telling which residues of a chain are disordered
(Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}b). The three Linker specialists sweep the pooled table at
\#1, \#2 and \#3 and sit at \#25, \#26 and \#29 within protein: excellent at
ranking whole chains by linker content, unremarkable at locating linkers inside
one.

Each disagreement is a certificate rather than an observation.
\code{inversion\_requires\_between\_gap} states that the pooled order reverses
the within-protein order only when $w_{\text{within}}\times$(within gap)
$< w_{\text{between}}\times$(between gap), so an inversion \emph{forces} a
between-protein gap at least $(w_{\text{within}}/w_{\text{between}})$ times the
within-protein one. On Disorder-NOX every certificate holds and each is
over-determined by 562--2305$\times$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}c). Calibration
differences of ordinary size do not merely outweigh discrimination differences
of ordinary size; they swamp them by three orders of magnitude.

The counterweight belongs here and not in a footnote: Spearman(pooled, within)
is $+0.964$ on Disorder-PDB and never below $+0.849$, so the decomposition does
not overturn CAID3 (Fig.~\ref{fig:rescored}a). It locates where the two
orderings disagree, and the movement is large where they do.

\subsection*{A benchmark can order at most $\lceil 1/2\eps\rceil$ methods}

\code{BenchmarkCapacity.card\_le\_benchCapacity} (Lean~4, \code{sorry}-free;
axioms \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice}, \code{Quot.sound}). A benchmark
with $n$ targets, scores averaged into $[0,1]$ and therefore on the grid of
denominator $n$, can place in a certified order at most
\[
  k(n,\eps,\delta) \;=\; \frac{n}{\lfloor c\,n\rfloor + 1} + 1,
  \qquad c = \max(\delta,\, 2\eps)
\]
methods, \emph{however many are entered}; $\delta$ is the smallest difference
worth calling a difference ($\delta = 0$ throughout, and any positive $\delta$
only lowers $k$). \code{benchCapacity\_attained} exhibits a family of exactly
$k$ methods the benchmark does resolve, in each of three score models, so $k$ is
the capacity and not an estimate, and \code{benchCapacity\_noise\_only} gives the
$n$-free ceiling $k \le \lceil 1/2\eps \rceil$: collecting more items does not
buy resolution the labels lack.

$\eps$ is measured, not assumed. MobiDB\cite{mobidb} publishes the
missing-residue call of each deposited structure, up to 806 for one protein.
Across 160 CAID3 Disorder-PDB targets, 4{,}886 of 61{,}013 evidenced residues
are context-dependent --- missing in some structures and observed in others ---
giving $\eps = 0.0801$; an independent estimator over all pairs of structures of
the same protein gives 0.0651. That is not measurement error but the fact that
the label is not a function of the sequence: a region ordered in one crystal
form and not another is scored as one number per residue against a reference
that depends on which structure was consulted.

Three independent routes --- the target grid, the residue-level form
\code{card\_le\_capacity\_labelNoise}, and the $n$-free ceiling --- give a
capacity of \textbf{seven} on every benchmark of two CAID rounds
(Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}a; Supplementary Table~S6). CAID3 admits 117 entrants
and can place at most seven of them in a certified order. To order the full
field, the annotation error rate would have to fall to 0.427\%: a nineteen-fold
improvement in label quality, not a larger benchmark.

\textbf{This is impossibility, not low power.} A benchmark observes the
annotation, not the truth, and every labelling within the noise budget is a
consistent truth. For two methods whose measured scores are close,
\code{unresolvable\_pair} \emph{constructs two such truths}, one making each
strictly better, both compatible with everything recorded. The ordering is
therefore not a function of the data, and no analysis of those data recovers it
--- not a larger bootstrap, not permutation testing, not Monte Carlo. And
\code{over\_capacity\_has\_close\_pair} shows that once the entry list exceeds
capacity, such a pair necessarily exists.

Our own results are consistent with this, which is the point rather than an
excuse. Five pre-registered architectures and an ensemble each failed to
separate from PUNCH2 on pooled AUC, and each of the five also failed the
endpoint its own registration named against a regime-matched control, with none
of fifteen paired comparisons surviving Holm correction (Supplementary
Note~S4). We were trying to resolve a pair the benchmark cannot resolve.

\subsection*{The bound applies to the benchmarks the field uses}

Nothing in \code{card\_le\_benchCapacity} is about proteins. Top-1 accuracy is an
average over $n$ items on the grid $1/n$: flipping one label moves it by $1/n$,
so a wrong fraction $\eps$ moves it by at most $\eps$, and two models must
differ by more than $2\eps$ before the observation certifies their order. The
theorem applies verbatim (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}d; Supplementary Table~S17):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
benchmark & test set & $\eps$ & capacity \\
\midrule
QuickDraw & 50,426,266 & 10.12\% & \textbf{5} \\
CAID3 (residue labels) & 99,239 & 8.01\% & \textbf{7} \\
CIFAR-100 & 10,000 & 5.85\% & \textbf{9} \\
ImageNet & 50,000 & 5.83\% & \textbf{9} \\
Amazon Reviews & 9,996,437 & 3.90\% & 13 \\
IMDB & 25,000 & 2.90\% & 18 \\
Caltech-256 & 29,780 & 1.54\% & 33 \\
AudioSet & 20,371 & 1.35\% & 38 \\
20news & 7,532 & 1.09\% & 46 \\
CIFAR-10 & 10,000 & 0.54\% & 93 \\
MNIST & 10,000 & 0.15\% & 334 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\textbf{ImageNet can place nine models in a certified order.} Its leaderboard
ranks on the order of a thousand. The published rates are lower bounds, so these
capacities are upper bounds; and the theorem allows the full score range
$[0,1]$, whereas published models occupy a far narrower band, so the realisable
figure is smaller again --- of the order of four for ImageNet over the range
modern models actually span.

This does not say benchmarking is futile. It says a benchmark should publish its
capacity alongside its ranking, and stop printing places it cannot support.

Sampling error in $\eps$ propagates to the capacity, and we carry it two ways.
Bootstrapping our estimate over the 159 proteins gives $\eps = 0.0649$, 95\% CI
$[0.0497, 0.0814]$, and a capacity of 8, 95\% CI $[7, 11]$; the pairwise rate
gives 52, 95\% CI $[37, 119]$. Even at the pessimistic end of both, the pairwise
protocol resolves five times more of the field. The estimation step is also
formalised: \code{capacity\_or\_rare\_event} joins a one-sided tail bound to the
capacity theorem and \code{capacity\_at\_95\_confidence} states the instance ---
with 2{,}746 measured pairs, at least 160 disagreements has probability at most
$1/20$ under any true rate below 4\%, and at 4\% \emph{or above} a benchmark
certifiably orders at most 13 methods. The test is one-sided by construction, since a measurement
can only lower-bound the noise rate, which is the direction that shrinks
capacity.

How much of the field this leaves undecidable is counted rather than asserted.
\code{card\_closePairs\_lower} is a pigeonhole on score blocks with a
Cauchy--Schwarz step, and \code{unresolvable\_count\_117} gives the instance: a
capacity of 8 against 117 entrants forces \textbf{at least 798} of the 6{,}786
pairwise comparisons to be unresolvable --- not unresolved by this analysis, but
undecidable from these data by anyone.

\subsection*{Pairwise scoring makes the noise second-order}

The bound constrains \emph{labels}. AUC is a function of ordered \emph{pairs},
and the noise a ranking statistic suffers is the rate at which the annotation
\emph{reverses} a pair.

Let $T$ be the truth on one protein and $L$ the annotation. A pair $(p,q)$ is
discordant when $T$ orders it one way and $L$ the other, which requires
$T(p){=}1, L(p){=}0$ \emph{and} $T(q){=}0, L(q){=}1$: both residues flip, in
opposite directions. With $d = |T \setminus L|$ and $u = |L \setminus T|$,
\[
  \mathrm{discordant}(T,L) = 2\,d\,u \quad\text{exactly},
  \qquad \mathrm{noise}(T,L) = d+u ,
\]
and by AM--GM $\mathrm{discordant} \le \mathrm{noise}^2/2$. Both hold with no
hypotheses (\code{discordant\_eq\_flip\_product},
\code{discordant\_le\_noise\_sq}). The pairs a pairwise protocol can score are
those \emph{both} labellings order, counted exactly as well:
$|\mathrm{comparable}(T,L)| = 2(|T \cap L|\cdot|(T \cup L)^{c}| + du)$
(\code{card\_comparablePairs}), so the pairwise noise rate is exactly
$\eps_{\text{pair}} = du/(|T \cap L|\cdot|(T \cup L)^{c}| + du)$.
\code{noise\_orderKey} closes the loop: on comparable pairs that quantity
\emph{is} the label noise of the answer key the annotation induces, so the
capacity theorem applies at the pair level verbatim
(\code{pairwise\_capacity\_bound}).

Measured on 2{,}746 pairs of deposited structures of the same protein, across
the 159 CAID3 Disorder-PDB targets with at least two (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}b;
Supplementary Table~S7):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & pooled & median protein \\
\midrule
label-flip rate $\eps_{\text{label}}$ & 0.0651 & 0.0342 \\
pairwise discordance $\eps_{\text{pair}}$ & \textbf{0.0100} & 0.0006 \\
capacity $\lceil 1/2\eps \rceil$ & \textbf{8 $\rightarrow$ 51} & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
A benchmark scored on within-protein pairwise comparisons can order 51 methods
where the residue-level protocol orders 8. CAID3 has 117 entrants.

\textbf{A closed form, and the hypothesis it does not need.} The published bound
$\eps_{\text{pair}} \le 2\eps^2$ requires the \emph{agreement} classes to
balance, $|T \cap L| = |(T \cup L)^{c}|$, and on a reference that is 31.6\%
disordered that fails comprehensively: pooled, the agreement classes stand at
240{,}506 disordered against 555{,}402 ordered, and the condition holds on 32 of
2{,}746 structure pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}c). The bound holds on all 32 of
those, and the pooled rate exceeds it --- the hypothesis is load-bearing, not
decorative, because the denominator collapses when the classes are lopsided.

\code{nuPair\_le\_imbalanced} removes it. Writing
$\kappa = (|T \cap L| + |(T \cup L)^{c}|)^2 / (4\,|T \cap L|\,|(T \cup L)^{c}|)$
for the imbalance factor,
\[
  \eps_{\text{pair}} \;\le\; \kappa\,\eps^2 / (1-\eps)^2 ,
\]
assuming only that both agreement classes are nonempty --- no balance condition,
no bound on the noise rate. \code{one\_le\_kappa} and
\code{kappa\_eq\_one\_iff\_balanced} pin $\kappa$ down, and the published bound is
now a corollary rather than a separate result. Checked on every CAID3 structure
pair where it is defined, it holds on \textbf{2{,}266 of 2{,}266}. A benchmark
can therefore predict its own pairwise gain from its label-noise rate and its
class prevalence without measuring pair discordance first, which is what makes
the protocol adoptable by benchmarks that cannot measure $\eps_{\text{pair}}$ at
all.

\subsection*{The escape, measured on ImageNet}

The taxonomy is testable on ImageNet itself. Northcutt \emph{et al.} released,
for every flagged item, the given label, the proposed correction and the votes
of the annotators who adjudicated it --- exactly the $(T, L)$ pair the
discordance identity needs, with $L$ the annotation the benchmark ships and $T$
the annotation after correction. So the same test set can be re-scored on a
statistic that \emph{does} have the pairing structure: per-class one-vs-rest
AUC, averaged over classes, where each class is a group and the metric depends
only on the ranking of images within it.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
 & $\eps_{\text{label}}$ & $\eps_{\text{pair}}$ & capacity, top-1 & capacity, per-class AUC \\
\midrule
ImageNet  & 0.0512 & $1.3\times10^{-6}$ & \textbf{10} & \textbf{387,425} \\
CIFAR-100 & 0.0421 & $1.8\times10^{-5}$ & 12 & 27,337 \\
CIFAR-10  & 0.0029 & $7.1\times10^{-7}$ & 173 & 701,173 \\
MNIST     & 0.0013 & $5.6\times10^{-8}$ & 385 & 8,990,001 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Same items, same labels, same errors; four to five orders of magnitude in what
the benchmark can resolve, decided by the statistic alone. (Our adjudication
rule --- plurality of annotators, ties to the given label --- is stricter than
the published one, giving 2{,}561 validated ImageNet errors against their
2{,}916 and a top-1 capacity of 10 rather than 9; the conclusion is insensitive
to it.)

Two limits belong with this. \textbf{Capacity is necessary, not sufficient}: a
constant predictor has infinite capacity and no value, so the claim is only that
among statistics measuring the same ability one should prefer the one whose
noise is second-order. And on ImageNet we can demonstrate the capacity half but
not the other, because no comparable archive of many models' per-image
predictions exists. On CAID both halves are demonstrated on the same data: the
capacity rises from 8 to 51, \emph{and} the resulting ranking reproduces on a
held-out round at $\rho = 0.997$. That is what a benchmark with repeat
annotations buys, and it is why the protein case carries the argument.

\subsection*{A protocol, and the field re-scored}

\textbf{The CAID pairwise protocol} (Methods) scores a method by the
\emph{unweighted} mean of its per-target Mann--Whitney AUCs, over the targets
carrying both classes, among methods that predicted every evaluated residue of
every target. Two methods are reported as ordered only when a paired Wilcoxon
test over targets rejects equality after Holm correction, and the number of
methods the reference can order at all is
$\lceil 1/2\eps_{\text{pair}} \rceil$, with anything beyond that reported as an
unresolved group rather than as a rank.

The unweighted mean is what makes the protocol calibration-invariant --- each
per-target AUC is unchanged by any strictly monotone recalibration of that
target's scores, so any function of them is too --- and the capacity step is what
keeps it honest: a rank the labels cannot support is not printed.

Re-scoring CAID3 (Fig.~\ref{fig:rescored}; Supplementary Table~S3), the two
orderings agree overall ($\rho = 0.844$ over 357 method--benchmark pairs) and
disagree sharply in places. On Disorder-PDB PUNCH2 falls from 4th to 7th; on
Disorder-NOX \textbf{AlphaFold-pLDDT moves from 39th to 6th} --- a training-free
confidence score, never trained on disorder, sixth in the field at the
residue-level question and thirty-ninth on the number the challenge reports.

The protocol also declares what it cannot order. The leader is separated at the
Holm-corrected margin from 55 of 59 eligible methods on Disorder-PDB, 51 of 59
on Disorder-NOX, 56 of 71 on Binding, 50 of 71 on Binding-IDR and 87 of 92 on
Linker (Fig.~\ref{fig:rescored}c), against seven orderings in total for the
residue-level protocol. Those counts exceed the capacity of 51 and are allowed
to: the capacity bounds a certified \emph{pairwise separated family}
(\code{pairSeparated\_card\_le\_capacity}), whereas 87 separations against one
leader are 87 statements about the pairs $\{\text{leader}, X\}$, which need not
compose. \code{many\_separations\_short\_chain} makes the gap concrete with a
two-cluster instance certifying $2k^2$ ordered comparisons in which no separated
subfamily exceeds two members. Step 6 governs the ranking the protocol prints,
not the number of pairs a test rejects.

\textbf{The extra resolution is not noise.} Fifty-seven methods entered both
CAID2 and CAID3; ranking them on CAID3 under each protocol and asking which
reproduces their CAID2 ranking gives $\rho = 0.997$ for pairwise against 0.976
for pooled on Disorder-PDB, and 0.83--0.997 against 0.82--0.95 across four
benchmarks (Fig.~\ref{fig:rescored}d). The pairwise ranking transfers as well as
the pooled one. It is \emph{not} shown to be more valid and we do not claim it
is --- three of four benchmarks show no significant difference and one favours
the pooled protocol. The case rests on capacity, not on a validity advantage the
data do not show.

\subsection*{An operating guarantee, and the price of it}

Every disorder predictor in CAID reports a ranking statistic; none answers the
question a biologist has, \emph{how often will this call be wrong?} Conformal
risk control supplies the missing guarantee, assuming nothing about the model,
the distribution or the calibration of the scores, and its validity is proved
here (\code{crc\_expected\_risk\_le}) rather than cited --- as is its honest
companion, \code{grouped\_validity\_underdetermines\_element\_coverage}, which is
why the per-residue shortfall we measure (0.876 against a 0.90 target) is
reported as a measurement and never as a promise.

Applied to all 70 full-coverage CAID3 Disorder-PDB methods
(Fig.~\ref{fig:operating}): validity is free and identical for everyone, and the
entire content is the price, which runs from 32.0\% of the protein flagged to
97.3\%. Buying the same guarantee with a per-protein quantile instead of a global
threshold prices discrimination alone rather than discrimination and
calibration, and the difference is what protein-level calibration is worth
operationally --- the leaders lean on it twice as hard as we do. That yields the
sharpest comparison in the paper: on Disorder-PDB pooled AUC separates
DisorderNet from PUNCH2 by $+0.0043$ at $p = 0.20$, a difference we have never
been able to call real, while the calibration-invariant operating cost separates
them by 5.3 points and by 10.2 on Disorder-NOX. \emph{The two are statistically
inseparable on the benchmark's own metric and are not close as instruments.}

\subsection*{The unit of testing is a design choice too}

The same structure appears downstream, when a predictor is run over a proteome
and asked which regions are disordered. A screen's candidates share a calibration
run, a model and a training set, so under arbitrary dependence
Benjamini--Hochberg\cite{bh} needs the harmonic correction\cite{by}. We prove it
from scratch in a finite setting, assuming only that a true null's p-value is
valid (\code{selfConsistent\_fdr\_le\_harmonic}), and prove it cannot be
lowered: an explicit joint law attains $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{FDP}] = qH_m$ exactly
(\code{bh\_fdr\_eq\_harmonic}), so uncorrected BH exceeds its nominal level from
two candidates on (Fig.~\ref{fig:screen}a,c). The screen's one remaining
assumption is discharged too: \code{conformalP\_superuniform} proves a
split-conformal p-value superuniform from exchangeability alone, and
\code{conformal\_screen\_fdr\_le} composes the two --- exchangeable scores in,
false discovery rate at most $\alpha$ out, under arbitrary dependence. The
dependence is not incidental:
\code{conformalP\_dependent\_of\_shared\_calibration} records that conformal
p-values are coupled \emph{by construction}, every candidate being compared
against the same calibration set, which is why an arbitrary-dependence theorem
is the correct tool rather than a conservative one.

The factor therefore depends on one thing: how many hypotheses the screen states.
And disorder is a property of a region. \code{fdp\_lift} shows a region-level
report and its residue-level reading have the \emph{same} false discovery
proportion, so \code{region\_screen\_fdr\_le} gives residue-level control at
$\alpha$ from a region-level procedure at $\alpha/H_M$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:screen}b).
Running it on CAID3 Disorder-PDB, the region-level screen reports 2{,}942
residues at a realised false discovery proportion of 0.024 and power 0.568,
against 203 residues and power 0.036 for the residue-level screen at the same
guaranteed rate: \textbf{14.5$\times$ the discoveries}. A third constraint
dominates both and is absent from the theory --- a conformal p-value cannot fall
below $1/(n_{\text{cal}}{+}1)$, so no discovery is possible unless
$n_{\text{cal}} + 1 \ge m H_m/\alpha$, \emph{linear} in the number of hypotheses
where the correction is only logarithmic. Supplementary Note~S7.

\subsection*{DisorderNet}

DisorderNet is a frozen-backbone, low-capacity multitask head over
ESM-2 650M\cite{esm2} with an AlphaFold\cite{af2} structure channel block
($\sim$2M trainable parameters; Methods). The pre-registered model of record
ranks first of 115 entrants on three of five CAID3 references and first among
full-coverage entrants on all four CAID2 references, at coverage 1.00 throughout
(Fig.~\ref{fig:model}b). Of the registered primary family, P1 (beat
AlphaFold-rsa) is confirmed at Holm-adjusted $p = 0.0252$ and P2 (beat PUNCH2)
is not significant ($+0.0043$, $p = 0.2022$); superiority on pooled AUC is not
claimed.

On the calibration-invariant axis it beats the CAID3 Disorder-PDB winner on
\emph{all five} benchmarks, per-target paired Wilcoxon, Holm-corrected across
the full family of thirty comparisons (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a), from Linker
$+0.1364$ ($p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$) to Binding-IDR $+0.2253$ ($p = 0.050$).

A temporal holdout tests generalisation without relying on any filter being
correct. Of 1{,}916 protein polymer entities released after the training caches
were built, 186 survive removal of exact training sequences and BLAST homologues
at $\ge 40\%$ identity --- 71\% of the ``new'' chains were already represented
(Fig.~\ref{fig:model}c), so a temporal cutoff alone is not a leak control. On
the 61 chains where the training-free structural baselines can also be computed,
the model reaches 0.9095 pooled and 0.8023 within-protein against
AlphaFold-pLDDT's 0.8737 and 0.7816 (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}d, matched sets).

The holdout also settles a question CAID3 cannot. Five pre-registered variants
were inseparable from their control on the benchmark, and the capacity result
says it could not have separated them whatever they did --- so the natural
objection is that real improvements are being hidden. New data is not bounded by
CAID3's capacity, and on these 186 unseen chains \emph{none} of the four
scorable variants beats its regime-matched control, on pooled AUC ($-0.0033$ to
$-0.0086$), pair-weighted within-protein AUC ($-0.0047$ to $-0.0301$) or the
unweighted form ($-0.0075$ to $-0.0137$). There was nothing to resolve. And the
unflattering result the set exists to produce: the protein-bias variant is our
best CAID3 model and the worst of three here, the only one failing to separate
from a training-free baseline.

\section*{Discussion}
% =====================================================================

The capacity bound is the result we would most like to see used, because it is
not about disorder and not about any one benchmark. Any benchmark that ranks
methods on annotations of finite accuracy has a capacity, it is
$\lceil 1/2\eps \rceil$ regardless of how many items are scored, and past it the
ordering is not a function of the observations --- \code{unresolvable\_pair}
constructs, for any two close methods, two ground truths consistent with
everything recorded, one favouring each. Of the ten benchmarks whose label-error
rates have been measured, every one is over its capacity and most by two orders
of magnitude.

The objection that nothing can then be ranked is answered by the statistic. A
pair reverses only when both its members are mis-annotated in opposite
directions, so a ranking statistic over within-group pairs suffers second-order
noise where an average of per-item losses suffers first-order. The same ImageNet
validation set, the same labels and the same adjudicated errors give a capacity
of ten under top-1 accuracy and $3.9\times10^{5}$ under per-class ranking; and
how much any benchmark would gain follows from its label-noise rate and class
prevalence alone (\code{nuPair\_le\_imbalanced}), so the calculation can be done
before anything is adopted. Capacity is necessary and not sufficient --- a
constant predictor has infinite capacity and no value --- so the claim is that
among statistics measuring the same ability one should prefer the one whose
noise is second-order. CAID is where both halves are demonstrated together,
because proteins are solved more than once: capacity rises from 8 to 51
\emph{and} the ranking reproduces on a held-out round at $\rho = 0.997$. Most
benchmarks cannot check their own resolution, which is an argument for building
repeat annotations rather than more items.

The screening result is the same principle downstream: a deflation factor set by
how many hypotheses are stated, attained and so not arguable away, and a report
whose false discovery proportion does not change with the unit it is read at.
\textbf{Where labels are noisy or tests are many, what a study can resolve
depends on a unit that is usually chosen by convention rather than by argument.}
We recommend that a benchmark publish its capacity beside its ranking, stop
printing places it cannot support, and where its statistic admits the pairing
structure, score pairs. CAID4's references are unpublished, so all three are
adoptable now.

\textbf{What we do not claim.} The bound does not say a published ranking is
wrong --- it says the data cannot establish it, and if label errors shifted every
model equally the ordering would survive. That is an assumption about the
errors, and Northcutt \emph{et al.} measured the opposite. On ImageNet we
demonstrate the capacity half but not the reproducibility half, for want of an
archive of many models' per-image predictions. The screen is a demonstration on
a public benchmark, not a biological discovery. DisorderNet is not state of the
art on the metric CAID reports: $+0.0043$ over PUNCH2 at $p = 0.20$, and no
framing makes that a win. Finally, several of this project's own results were
wrong and were caught by measurement rather than review --- including, while this
manuscript was written, a headline capacity of 72 that formalising the
denominator turned into 51 (Supplementary Note~S5). A benchmark culture that
reports only the number cannot catch any of them.
